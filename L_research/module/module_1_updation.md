# Alpha Blocks Teaching Method: Sound-Based Word Building
## Lesson Plan Based on Parent Q&A Session

## Learning Objectives

They should be able to pronounce:
- Recognize and pronounce the sounds: ba, na, tha, ga, ma, and shh
- Identify these sounds in isolation and within words

## Alpha Blocks Breakdown

### Core Sounds to Master

| Sound | Letter(s) | Example Words | Description |
|-------|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| **ba** | B, b | bat, bad, bag, ban, ball | Voiced bilabial sound - touch both lips |
| **na** | N, n | nap, not, nut, net, nod | Nasal sound - air through nose |
| **tha** | Th, th | that, this, them, the, thin | Dental fricative - tongue between teeth |
| **ga** | G, g | gap, gas, got, gun, gum | Voiced velar sound - back of throat |
| **ma** | M, m | mat, mad, man, map, mom | Nasal bilabial sound - closed lips |
| **shh** | Sh, sh | shop, shut, ship, show, shy | Voiceless fricative - challenging but important |


## Teaching Methodology

### Phase 1: Sound Introduction (Week 1-2)

#### Step 1: Isolated Sound Practice
- **Display one alpha block** at a time
- **Model the sound**: Make the sound slowly and clearly
- **Have students repeat**: 3-5 times per sound
- **Use hand/mouth positioning**: Show how lips and tongue move
- **Connect to familiar objects**: Associate sounds with things they know



#### Step 2: Sound Recognition
- **Listen and Point**: Call out the sound, students point to correct alpha block
- **Sound Hunt**: Find the sound in picture cards

---

### Phase 2: Sound Blending (Week 3-4)

#### Step 1: CVC Word Building
Build simple three-letter words by blending sounds together:

**Using BA:**
- ba + t = **bat**
- ba + d = **bad**
- ba + g = **bag**
- ba + n = **ban**
- ba + ll = **ball**

**Using NA:**
- na + p = **nap**
- na + t = **not**
- na + il = **nail**

**Using GA:**
- ga + p = **gap**
- ga + s = **gas**
- ga + t = **got**
- ga + g = **gag**

**Using MA:**
- ma + t = **mat**
- ma + d = **mad**
- ma + n = **man**
- ma + p = **map**

**Using THA:**
- tha + t = **that**
- tha + is = **this**
- tha + em = **them**

#### Step 2: Blending Process
1. **Show the alpha blocks** in sequence
2. **Say each sound separately**: "ba... t"
3. **Blend them together smoothly**: "bat"
4. **Say the whole word**: "bat"
5. **Reinforce meaning**: "A bat is used to hit a ball"

---

### Phase 3: Special Focus - The "SHH" Sound

#### Why "Shh" is Challenging:
- It's a **digraph** (two letters making one sound)
- Requires precise tongue/teeth positioning
- Less intuitive than single consonants
- Often mispronounced as "s" sound

#### Teaching "Shh" Step-by-Step:

**Step 1: Sound Isolation**
- Model the sound clearly: "Shhhhhh" (like asking someone to be quiet)
- Show teeth together, lips slightly rounded
- Have them feel the air coming out
- Practice in front of a mirror

**Step 2: Exaggerated Movement**
- Make the "shh" sound while showing the mouth position
- Compare it to the "s" sound - show the difference
- Repeat 5-10 times for muscle memory

**Step 3: Word Building with "Shh"**
- Start with simple words:
  - shh + op = **shop**
  - shh + ut = **shut**
  - shh + ip = **ship**
  - shh + ow = **show**
  - shh + e = **she**

**Step 4: Blending Practice**
- Go SLOWLY with "shh" words
- Have them hold the "shh" sound longer
- Blend gradually: "shhh... op = shop"

**Step 5: Repetition and Reinforcement**
- Use the same "shh" words daily
- Practice pronunciation multiple times per week
- Use visual cues and gestures

## Building from Sounds

### The Sound-to-Spelling Process

```
SOUND → SAY → BLEND → SPELL → WRITE → READ
  ↓       ↓      ↓       ↓      ↓      ↓
 /ba/   "ba"   "ba+t"   bat    bat    bat
```

**Step-by-step spelling model:**

1. **Say the word aloud**: "I want to spell 'bat'"
2. **Break into sounds**: "bat = /ba/ /t/"
3. **Identify each sound**: "First sound is 'ba', next is 't'"
4. **Write each letter/combination**: b-a-t
5. **Read back**: "bat" - check pronunciation
